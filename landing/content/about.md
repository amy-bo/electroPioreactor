[AMYBO](https://amybo.org) created the electroPioreactor to make it possible to produce hydrogen-oxidising bacteria (HOB) affordably, safely and locally, as a sustainable alternative protein.

The electroPioreactor was initially developed by AMYBO volunteers, principally Martin Currie and Gerrit Niezen, co-founder of [LabCrafter](https://labcrafter.co.uk), UK distributors of [Pioreactor](https://pioreactor.com), the open-source bioreactor on which it is based.

## The CARMA Hub Pump Priming project

The Aseptic electroPioreactor (AEP) is funded by [CARMA Hub Pump Priming](https://carmahub.co.uk/about-us/pump-priming-projects/#:~:text=Affordable%20Aseptic%20Electro-Bioreactor%20for%20Reproducible%20Hydrogen%20Oxidising%20Bacteria%20(HOB)%20Research). The aim is a low-cost bioreactor that keeps a single strain free of contamination, so HOB research can be repeated from lab to lab.

## History

- **2023 and 2024.** The AMYBO community's first experiments: a Pioreactor with an electrode pushed through a 3D-printed cap, and a CO₂ line from a SodaStream cylinder.
- **2025.** The first Aseptic electroPioreactors. Imperial and Edinburgh students trained on them in Edinburgh in November.
- **2026.** AEP0.2, the electroPioreactor plugin and the [Assembly Video Guide](https://docs.electropioreactor.org/AEP/). Two Mixed-culture electroPioreactors went to MSc projects at the University of Edinburgh.

## Open source

The designs, parts lists, guide, media and gas model are all in one public repository, on [GitHub](https://github.com/amy-bo/electroPioreactor) and on [Radicle](https://app.radicle.xyz/nodes/iris.radicle.xyz/rad%3AzY4GuyErQo91xGYdLi4r2bea9QDJ). The [plugin](https://github.com/amy-bo/pioreactor-electroPioreactor-plugin) has its own repository and is released under the MIT License.
