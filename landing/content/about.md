[AMYBO](https://amybo.org) created the electroPioreactor to make it possible to produce hydrogen-oxidising bacteria (HOB) affordably, safely and locally, as a sustainable alternative protein. It was initially developed by AMYBO volunteers, principally Dr Martin Currie and Dr Gerrit Niezen, co-founder of [LabCrafter](https://labcrafter.co.uk), UK distributors of [Pioreactor](https://pioreactor.com), the open-source bioreactor on which it is based.

## The CARMA Hub Pump Priming project

The Aseptic electroPioreactor (AEP) was funded by [CARMA Hub Pump Priming](https://carmahub.co.uk/about-us/pump-priming-projects/#:~:text=Affordable%20Aseptic%20Electro-Bioreactor%20for%20Reproducible%20Hydrogen%20Oxidising%20Bacteria%20(HOB)%20Research). The aim was a low-cost bioreactor that keeps a single strain free of contamination, so HOB research could be repeated from lab to lab.

## History

- **2023 and 2024.** The AMYBO community's first experiments: a Pioreactor with various electrodes and vial cap designs, and a CO₂ line from a SodaStream cylinder.
- **2025.** The first Aseptic electroPioreactors (AEP0.1 and AEP0.1.1). Imperial and Edinburgh students trained on them in Edinburgh in November.
- **2026.** Teo Reid’s MRes thesis (Imperial College London); the second main prototype, AEP0.2, with the electroPioreactor plugin and the [Assembly Video Guide](https://docs.electropioreactor.org/AEP/). Two Mixed-culture electroPioreactors went to MSc projects at the University of Edinburgh. Work continues with Nelly Oresharova’s PhD project at Imperial College London, Bingqiao Zhao’s PhD project at the University of Edinburgh and AMYBO volunteers Dr Martin Currie and Dr Gerrit Niezen running HOB experiments using AEP0.2s.

## Open source

The designs, parts lists, guide, media and gas model are all in one public repository, on [GitHub](https://github.com/amy-bo/electroPioreactor) and on [Radicle](https://app.radicle.xyz/nodes/iris.radicle.xyz/rad%3AzY4GuyErQo91xGYdLi4r2bea9QDJ). The [plugin](https://github.com/amy-bo/pioreactor-electroPioreactor-plugin) has its own repository and is released under the MIT License.
